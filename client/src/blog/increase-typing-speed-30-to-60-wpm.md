# How to Improve Typing Speed from 30 to 60 WPM

Typing is an important skill to have, whether you're a student, professional, programmer, writer, or anyone who uses computers regularly. By improving your typing speed, you can complete everyday tasks much quicker than before.

However, sometimes you can get stuck at around 30 words per minute. You may feel like you're practicing regularly but aren't seeing much improvement. Maybe you're still looking down at your keyboard, making plenty of mistakes, or struggling to get into a rhythm.

The good news is that improving your typing speed is possible with the right techniques and regular practice.

This article will show you how to improve your typing speed from **30 WPM to 60 WPM**. We'll also look at how to improve your typing accuracy, common mistakes to avoid, and a simple four-week plan you can follow.

## 1. Typing Speed: 30 WPM vs 60 WPM

WPM stands for **Words Per Minute**. It tells us how many standardised words we can type in one minute.

Most typing tests use five characters, including spaces, as one standard word.

Here is a general idea of different typing speed levels:

| Typing Speed | Typing Level | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 10–20 WPM | Beginner | Still learning where the keys are |
| 20–30 WPM | Developing | Can type basic sentences |
| 30–40 WPM | Intermediate | Can type comfortably |
| 40–50 WPM | Good | Can complete many typing tasks quickly |
| 50–60 WPM | Strong | Can type comfortably for longer periods |
| 60–80 WPM | Advanced | Can type quickly and confidently |
| 80+ WPM | Very Fast | Can type very quickly with high accuracy |

These are just general ranges, and there isn't an official standard for everyone. A good typing speed depends on what you do, how much experience you have, how accurate you are, and the type of text you're typing.

### What Can You Do at 30–60 WPM?

Moving from 30 WPM to 60 WPM means doubling your typing speed.

At 30 WPM, you can type approximately 150 standardised words' worth of characters per minute. At 60 WPM, that increases to approximately 300.

This can help you save time when typing documents, completing assignments, or working with large amounts of text.

## 2. Benefits of Improving Your Typing Speed

Improving your typing speed can help you use your computer more efficiently.

Some of the main benefits include:

### Saving Time

If you're typing a long assignment or report, someone typing at 60 WPM will generally be able to enter the same text much quicker than someone typing at 30 WPM, assuming they have a similar level of accuracy.

### Improving Productivity

By typing faster, you can complete emails, notes, reports, and messages more quickly. This gives you more time to focus on the actual task instead of spending too much time typing.

### Typing With Confidence

Knowing where the keys are without looking down will help you type more smoothly. You can focus on what you're thinking rather than constantly searching for letters.

### Better Career Opportunities

Typing is useful in many careers. Data entry, administration, customer support, content writing, programming, and office roles all involve typing. Some employers also test typing ability during recruitment.

## 3. 7 Tips to Improve Typing Speed from 30 to 60 WPM

You don't need an expensive keyboard to improve your typing speed. Try following these simple tips:

### Tip 1: Learn the Correct Finger Positions

One of the most important things you can do to improve your typing speed is learn the correct finger positions.

Most typing systems start with **home row typing**.

- **Left hand:** A, S, D, F
- **Right hand:** J, K, L, ;
- **Thumbs:** Spacebar

Your index fingers should rest on the F and J keys. Most keyboards have small bumps on these keys to help you find the correct position without looking.

Each finger is responsible for reaching certain keys. Learning these positions helps reduce unnecessary hand movements and makes typing feel more natural.

Try placing your fingers on the home row and typing these simple combinations:

```text
asdf jkl;

fjdksl

sad lad fall
```

Take your time and make sure your fingers are in the right positions.

### Tip 2: Don't Look Down at Your Keyboard

Looking down after typing a few letters can disrupt your typing flow.

You'll also become dependent on searching for keys rather than developing muscle memory.

Try to keep your eyes on the screen while typing. You may make more mistakes and type slowly at first, but that's completely normal.

You can use a keyboard cover or another comfortable method to reduce the temptation to look at the keys. Don't use anything that makes typing uncomfortable.

The aim is to train your fingers to remember where the keys are.

### Tip 3: Focus on Accuracy First

One of the biggest mistakes beginners make is trying to type as fast as possible.

Imagine typing at 60 WPM but making lots of mistakes. You may actually spend more time correcting those mistakes than someone typing accurately at 45 WPM.

Try focusing on accuracy before increasing your speed.

Here are a few things to remember:

- Try to maintain at least 95% accuracy during practice.
- If you're making the same mistakes repeatedly, slow down.
- Take note of the letters you commonly mistype.
- Pay attention to your errors instead of ignoring them.

Once you're typing accurately and consistently, start increasing your speed gradually.

### Tip 4: Practice Every Day

It's generally better to do a short practice session every day rather than one very long session once a week.

Try following this simple routine:

| Activity | Time |
|---|---:|
| Home Row Practice | 2 minutes |
| Common Word Practice | 5 minutes |
| Sentence Practice | 5 minutes |
| Timed Typing Test | 3 minutes |
| Review Mistakes | 3 minutes |
| **Total** | **18 minutes** |

You can change this routine to suit your schedule.

The main thing is to be consistent. Regular practice gives you more opportunities to improve your finger movements, accuracy, and typing rhythm.

### Tip 5: Sit Comfortably

The way you sit and position your hands can affect your typing comfort.

Follow these simple suggestions:

- Sit back with good posture and proper support.
- Keep your shoulders relaxed.
- Ensure your elbows are in a comfortable position near your sides.
- Keep your wrists in a natural position.
- Don't press the keys too hard.
- Position your screen at a comfortable height so you don't constantly bend your neck.

Take short breaks while practicing. If you're experiencing constant pain or discomfort while typing, stop and reconsider your posture and setup.

### Tip 6: Practice With Real Words

Practicing random letters can help you learn where the keys are, but practicing with real words is equally important.

Try practicing common words such as:

- People
- Computer
- Technology
- Practice
- Keyboard
- Learning
- Development
- Productivity

Once you're comfortable typing these words, try practicing complete sentences.

This will help you become more comfortable with spaces, punctuation, capital letters, and normal typing patterns.

### Tip 7: Practice With Typing Tests

Without measuring your progress, you won't know how much you've improved.

Practice using a one-minute or three-minute typing test and record your results.

Here is an example of how your progress tracking could look:

| Day | Typing Speed | Accuracy |
|---|---:|---:|
| Day 1 | 30 WPM | 91% |
| Day 7 | 34 WPM | 93% |
| Day 14 | 40 WPM | 95% |
| Day 21 | 47 WPM | 96% |
| Day 28 | 52 WPM | 97% |

These numbers are just an example. You may improve faster or slower depending on your current skills and how regularly you practice.

Try tracking both your typing speed and accuracy. Over time, you should be able to notice gradual improvements.

## 4. A Four-Week Plan to Reach 60 WPM

Here is a simple four-week plan to help you improve your typing skills.

These are suggested targets, not guaranteed results. Move forward at a pace that allows you to maintain good accuracy and comfortable typing technique.

### Week 1: Build a Solid Foundation

**Focus: Finger positions and accuracy**

The aim for this week is to learn the keyboard and develop correct finger movements.

Try doing the following every day:

| Activity | Time |
|---|---:|
| Home Row Practice | 5 minutes |
| Common Word Practice | 5 minutes |
| Short Typing Tests | 5 minutes |
| Review Mistakes | 3 minutes |
| **Total** | **18 minutes** |

Aim to become comfortable typing around your current speed while improving your accuracy towards 95%.

Don't worry about increasing your speed too much during this week. Focus on building good typing habits.

### Week 2: Develop Consistency

**Focus: Touch typing and smooth movements**

Now that you're familiar with the finger positions, start practicing without looking at the keyboard.

Daily activities:

- Practice common words and letter combinations.
- Type complete sentences.
- Take one-minute typing tests.
- Identify the keys that cause the most mistakes.

**Suggested target:** Work towards approximately 35–40 WPM, depending on your starting point.

### Week 3: Gradually Increase Your Speed

**Focus: Typing rhythm and longer practice sessions**

During this week, start practicing longer sentences and paragraphs.

Daily activities:

- Complete two-minute or three-minute typing tests.
- Practice punctuation and capital letters.
- Try maintaining a steady typing rhythm.
- Review your mistakes after every test.

**Suggested target:** Work towards approximately 40–50 WPM.

Remember, accuracy is still important. Don't sacrifice it just to get a higher WPM score.

### Week 4: Improve Your Overall Performance

**Focus: Consistency and timed typing tests**

During the final week, try different typing exercises and test durations.

Daily activities:

- Complete a one-minute typing speed test.
- Try longer tests to build consistency.
- Practice commonly mistyped words.
- Compare your latest results with your first test.

**Suggested target:** Work towards 50–60 WPM while maintaining good accuracy.

You may not reach 60 WPM within these four weeks, and that's okay. Keep practicing until you can achieve your target consistently.

## 5. Best Typing Exercises to Improve Your Speed

Apart from regular typing tests, there are some simple exercises you can try to make your practice sessions more useful.

### Exercise 1: Home Row Practice

Practice typing these combinations:

```text
asdf jkl; asdf jkl;

a sad lad asks; all fall
```

This exercise is especially useful for beginners who are learning correct finger placement.

### Exercise 2: Common Word Practice

Choose 15–20 common words and type them repeatedly.

For example:

```text
people computer keyboard practice
technology learning development productivity
```

This helps you become familiar with common letter combinations.

### Exercise 3: Sentence Practice

Choose a short paragraph and type it several times.

Focus on maintaining a comfortable rhythm rather than trying to finish as quickly as possible.

### Exercise 4: One-Minute Typing Challenge

Set a one-minute timer and type a passage while maintaining good accuracy.

Record your WPM and accuracy after each attempt. Try repeating the exercise and see whether you can improve your result.

### Exercise 5: Practice Your Weak Keys

Review your typing mistakes and identify the letters you frequently mistype.

For example, if you often confuse R and T, practice words that contain those letters.

This makes your practice more useful because you're working directly on the areas where you need improvement.

## 6. Common Mistakes That Slow Down Your Typing Speed

While learning to type faster, try avoiding these common mistakes:

| Common Mistake | Better Approach |
|---|---|
| Looking at the keyboard repeatedly | Practice touch typing |
| Typing too fast and making errors | Focus on accuracy first |
| Using only two fingers | Learn proper finger placement |
| Pressing keys too hard | Use relaxed finger movements |
| Practicing irregularly | Follow a regular practice routine |
| Ignoring typing mistakes | Review weak keys and words |
| Comparing yourself with advanced typists | Track your own improvement |

Remember that improving your typing speed takes time. Don't become frustrated if your speed temporarily decreases while you're learning a better technique.

## 7. Track Your Typing Progress

A typing test helps you understand how your skills are changing over time.

There are two important measurements to keep in mind:

**Typing Speed (WPM):** The number of standardised words you can type per minute.

**Typing Accuracy:** The percentage of correctly typed characters or other units measured by a typing test.

For example, if your typing test shows:

- Typing Speed: 45 WPM
- Accuracy: 96%

It means you achieved a typing speed of 45 WPM with 96% accuracy according to that test's calculation method.

Different typing websites may calculate typing speed and accuracy differently. So, when comparing your results, try using the same typing test and settings each time.

**A useful tip:** Don't judge your progress using just one test. Take several tests and compare your average performance.
