# How to Touch Type

Have you ever seen someone typing really fast without even looking at their keyboard? They seem to be typing with their fingers on auto-pilot. This is known as Touch Typing and can be learnt by nearly anyone with regular practice.

Whether you're a student, coding, or working in an office environment, this can help speed up your typing and make using your computer easier.

We'll show you how to touch type, where your fingers should go, avoid any common mistakes, and set up a simple practice routine.

## What Is Touch Typing?

Touch Typing is when you use your fingers to find the keys on your keyboard instead of constantly looking down. Each finger is assigned to certain keys.

You'll learn where each key is in relation to each other and your fingers will begin to memorise where each key is.

Once you know where each key is, it becomes much easier to type common words like 'computer'.

The overall aim of Touch Typing is to type accurately and consistently without looking at your keyboard.

## 1. Know Where Your Fingers Go

The first thing you should learn when Touch Typing is your home row. These are the keys in the middle row of your keyboard.

On a standard QWERTY keyboard, your home row will be:

**A S D F | J K L ;**

**Left hand:**
- Little finger – A
- Ring finger – S
- Middle finger – D
- Index finger – F

**Right hand:**
- Index finger – J
- Middle finger – K
- Ring finger – L
- Little finger – ;

Both thumbs should be close to the space bar.

You may notice little bumps on the F and J keys. This helps you find your home row without looking at your keyboard.

![Home row finger placement chart](/home-row-guide.jpg)

*F and J have bumps to help you find the correct position.*

Take a few minutes to get used to putting your fingers in the right place. This will help with everything else.

## 2. Know Which Finger Goes Where

A common mistake beginners make when Touch Typing is using just their index fingers. While this may feel natural to start with, it won't be the fastest way to type.

Each finger has certain keys assigned to them:

**Left hand:**
- Little finger – Q/A/Z
- Ring finger – W/S/X
- Middle finger – E/D/C
- Index finger – R/T/F/G/V/B

**Right hand:**
- Index finger – Y/U/H/J/N/M
- Middle finger – I/K/,
- Ring finger – O/L/.
- Little finger – P and any nearby punctuation.

Depending on your keyboard, some punctuation may be assigned to different fingers.

Don't worry if it feels slow to start with. Just focus on using the right fingers.

## 3. Don't Look At Your Keyboard

This can be the hardest part for beginners.

When Touch Typing for the first time, you might find yourself looking down at your keyboard. Try to avoid doing this.

Just:

1. Look at your screen.
2. Put your fingers back in your home row.
3. Think about where the key is.
4. Press the key with the correct finger.
5. Check your screen.

You'll definitely make mistakes but this is normal. It will take time to learn where each key is by touch.

If needed, put a light cloth over your hands while practicing for a short period of time. Don't do this if it makes your hands uncomfortable.

## 4. Focus On Accuracy

A common mistake beginners make is focusing on getting a high WPM.

Typing quickly with lots of mistakes won't be very helpful. You'll spend a lot of time correcting your mistakes.

Focus on typing accurately.

If you can type at 25 WPM with 98% accuracy, you have a good base to work from. Focus on increasing your speed gradually instead of rushing to get to 60 WPM.

While practicing try to:
- Use the correct fingers.
- Make fewer mistakes.
- Type smoothly.
- Keep your hands and wrists relaxed.
- Type comfortably.

Accuracy comes first.

## 5. Practice For 15 Minutes Every Day

It's best to practice regularly instead of practicing for a few hours once a week. Practicing for 15 minutes a day can help you form good typing habits.

Practice routine for beginners:

**Minute 1-3: Home row.**
Practice typing `asdf`, `jkl;`, `asdf jkl;` and short home row words.

**Minute 4-7: Individual letters.**
Practice typing to the top and bottom rows. Make sure to put your fingers back in your home row.

**Minute 8-11: Common words.**
Type simple words like 'the', 'your', 'computer', 'school', 'keyboard' and 'practice'.

**Minute 12-15: Take a typing test.**
Take a short typing test. Try to focus on being accurate instead of getting a higher score than last time.

Try practicing for a week using this routine. You'll notice your fingers become more comfortable with the keyboard.

## 6. Common Mistakes To Avoid

As a beginner, you may form bad habits which will make it harder to improve.

- **Looking down too much:** This will stop you building confidence with knowing where the keys are.
- **Using the wrong fingers:** While it may feel easier to use just one or two fingers, it will make it harder to type efficiently.
- **Going too fast:** If you're going too fast, you won't be accurate which will cause extra corrections and can be frustrating.
- **Not practicing regularly:** Practicing for 15 minutes every day will be more helpful than practicing for one long time and then not practicing for a few days.
- **Poor posture:** Keep your shoulders relaxed, wrists comfortable and keyboard at a good height. Don't tense up.

## How Long Will It Take Me To Learn?

There isn't a set time as everyone will start at a different speed. Some people may become comfortable with the basics in a few days, but developing good Touch Typing habits can take a few weeks or longer.

The more regularly you practice with the correct technique, the faster you'll improve.

Don't compare your speed to experienced typists. Compare your current speed to your previous results.
